\begin{abstract}
This monograph defines a general relational tensor--scalar,
orbital--fractal algebra prior to any specific spacetime, metric, or physical
interpretation. The core signature contains relational admissibility, a
minimal point carrier, additive typed sectors, relational and tensor
products, contraction, typed transport, semantic coarse-graining, orbital
operators, and recursive branch refinement. Associativity, commutativity,
bilinearity, functoriality, equivariance, monoidality, and cross-operation
naturality are not silently imposed; their failures are retained as explicit
residuals.

Projective \(\CP\)/Fubini--Study geometry, scalar-angle Moir\'e rotation,
generic \(U(1)\) phase tools, and the Zero-Axis projective map are separated
from the core as reference realizations or bridge structures. In particular,
the recursive \(\CP\) construction is fibre-local rather than one global
Bloch sphere, and a phase representation of abstract relational holonomy must
be supplied by a concrete realization before the Zero-Axis lift is used.

The current canonical foundation remains \(\mathrm{RA0}+\mathrm{RR1}\);
the stronger one-axiom closure candidate \(\mathrm{RA0}^{*}\) is tracked
separately. No Planck scale, Minkowski structure, gravitational law, exact
self-similarity, or physical identification is assumed by the general
algebra.
\end{abstract}

\section{Scope}

The object developed here is not a spacetime model. It is a general algebra
of relations. Concrete geometric and physical constructions may later be
representations of this algebra, but they are not part of its definition.

The typed algebraic family is written
\[
\boxed{
\Arel=\left\{\mathcal A_{\sigma,n}^{p,q}\right\},
}
\]
where \((p,q)\) is tensor rank, \(n\in\mathbb N_0\) is refinement level,
and \(\sigma\in\Sigma\) is a semantic scale or context.

The scalar sector is not a separate ontology. It is the tensor rank-zero
sector:
\[
\boxed{
\mathbb K_{\sigma,n}=\mathcal A_{\sigma,n}^{0,0}.
}
\]

\section{Relational admissibility}

The foundational rule is stated as an admissibility condition:
\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Adm}(x)
\Longrightarrow
\exists\,y,r:\operatorname{Rel}_{r}(x,y).
}
\tag{RA0}
\]
An admissible observable is therefore not defined as an isolated object.
The statement is intentionally weaker and cleaner than a physical claim: it
does not assign a spacetime, energy, length, or dynamics.

A primitive relation is typed as a binary arrow
\[
\boxed{r:a\to b.}
\]
This is a definition of the primitive relational signature, not a theorem of
RA0. Higher-arity relations may be constructed separately from binary
relations or introduced by an explicit extension of the signature.

The absence of an admitted relation is not identified with algebraic zero.
For every typed sector there may be a zero element
\[
0_{\sigma,n}^{p,q}\in\mathcal A_{\sigma,n}^{p,q},
\]
while ``no relation'' is a separate condition.
In the additive reference sector this zero is executable as the identity
\[
X+0_X=X,
\qquad
X-X=0_X.
\]
The executable reference zero of an element preserves its rank, carrier,
semantic scale, and component dimension.

\section{Minimal carrier and minimal distinction}

The minimal carrier of the general algebra is a point
\[
\boxed{p.}
\]
No Planck length or other physical scale is attached to it at this level.
A later physical representation may provide such a scale through a separate
bridge.

The reference minimal two-channel realization of a distinction uses a
non-zero vector in \(\mathbb C^2\), modulo global phase:
\[
|\psi\rangle
=\alpha|0\rangle+\beta|1\rangle,
\qquad
|\alpha|^2+|\beta|^2=1,
\]
\[
\boxed{
\mathbb P(\mathbb C^2)=\CP\simeq S^2.
}
\tag{REF-CP1}
\]
The natural projective metric is Fubini--Study:
\[
\boxed{
d_{\FS}([\psi],[\phi])
=\arccos|\langle\psi|\phi\rangle|.
}
\]

A relation \(r\) may select two projective states
\[
r\longmapsto(p_r^-,p_r^+)\in\CP\times\CP.
\]
Maximal distinction is the special case
\[
\langle p_r^-|p_r^+\rangle=0,
\qquad
d_{\FS}=\frac{\pi}{2}.
\]
Generic relations need not be maximal and therefore need not select
antipodal Bloch points. Equation (REF-CP1) is a reference realization of a
complex binary distinction. It is not claimed to follow from RA0 alone.

\section{Recursive relational refinement}

A selected relational point may itself become the carrier of another
minimal distinction. Let
\[
V_n=\{-1,+1\}^{n}.
\]
For \(\omega\in V_n\), define the binary refinement
\[
\boxed{
p_\omega\longmapsto
\{p_{\omega-},p_{\omega+}\}.
}
\tag{RR1}
\]
RR1 is an explicit second foundational axiom: RA0 requires relation but does
not by itself force recursive refinement. Thus the general construction contains a recursive relational tower
\[
p\to\{p_-,p_+\}
\to\{p_{--},p_{-+},p_{+-},p_{++}\}
\to\cdots.
\]

Fractality means compatibility across refinement levels, not a requirement of
perfect geometric self-similarity. For a branch
\(\epsilon\in\{-1,+1\}\), use the typed refinement
\[
\boxed{
F_{\epsilon}:
\mathcal A_{\sigma,n}^{p,q}(p_{\omega})
\to
\mathcal A_{\tau,n+1}^{p,q}(p_{\omega\epsilon}).
}
\]
The target semantic scale \(\tau\) is explicit; the reference realization
preserves tensor rank and advances the relational carrier by exactly one
branch. For a relational product \(\star\), define
\[
\boxed{
\Delta_{F_{\epsilon}}(X,Y)
=
F_{\epsilon}(X\star Y)
-
F_{\epsilon}(X)\star F_{\epsilon}(Y).
}
\]
Exact self-similarity is the special case \(\Delta_F=0\). Non-zero residuals
remain legitimate algebraic information.

\subsection{Reference CP1 fibre realization of refinement}

The recursive relation admits a concrete projective reference realization
without promoting \(\CP\) to a universal axiom. Associate to each relational
carrier a local fibre
\[
\boxed{
\mathcal F_{\omega}\simeq\CP
}
\]
with its Fubini--Study metric. A local distinction
\[
D_{\omega}
=
\left(

\right),
\qquad
q_{\omega\pm}\in\mathcal F_{\omega},
\]
indexes the two child carriers
\[
p_{\omega-},
\qquad
p_{\omega+}.
\]
Each child carrier may then support its own local fibre
\[
\mathcal F_{\omega-}\simeq\CP,
\qquad
\mathcal F_{\omega+}\simeq\CP.

The selected projective state \(q_{\omega\pm}\) in the parent fibre is

\[
q_{\omega\pm}
\rightsquigarrow
p_{\omega\pm}
\rightsquigarrow
\mathcal F_{\omega\pm}.

This avoids treating all refinement levels as points on one global Bloch
sphere.

The executable maximal reference tree chooses an orthogonal pair in every
local fibre,
\[
d_{\FS}(q_{\omega-},q_{\omega+})=\frac{\pi}{2},
\]
but non-maximal local distinctions are also admissible. Hence maximal
antipodality belongs to one reference subclass, not to the general relational
axiom.

\section{Tensor--scalar grading and typed relations}

Each typed sector carries an additive module structure over a scalar domain \(\mathbb K\):
\[
\boxed{
(\mathcal A_{\sigma,n}^{p,q},+,0,-,\mathbb K\curvearrowright\mathcal A_{\sigma,n}^{p,q}).
}
\]
The abstract algebra does not fix \(\mathbb K=\mathbb C\); the reference kernel uses complex components. This additive structure is required before expressions such as commutators, associators, and refinement residuals can be formed as differences.

Elements are typed by tensor rank, semantic scale, and refinement level:
\[
X\in\mathcal A_{\sigma,n}^{p,q}.
\]
Tensor product and contraction have the schematic types
\[
\otimes:
\mathcal A_{\sigma,n}^{p,q}\times
\mathcal A_{\tau,m}^{r,s}
\to
\mathcal A_{\upsilon,k}^{p+r,q+s},
\]
\[
\operatorname{Contr}:
\mathcal A_{\sigma,n}^{p,q}
\to
\mathcal A_{\sigma,n}^{p-1,q-1},
\]
whenever the relevant type and relation constraints are satisfied.

The tensor product itself is supplied as a typed rule; the reference
Kronecker-style implementation is only one realization. Strict monoidal
properties are tested rather than assumed. Define
\[
\boxed{
\Delta_{\alpha}^{\otimes}(X,Y,Z)
=
\left\|
(X\otimes Y)\otimes Z
-
X\otimes(Y\otimes Z)
\right\|
}
\]
for strict associativity. For explicitly supplied left and right unit
candidates \(\mathbf 1_L,\mathbf 1_R\),
\[
\boxed{
\Delta_{\mathbf 1}^{\otimes}(X)
=
\max\left(
\|\mathbf 1_L\otimes X-X\|,
\|X\otimes\mathbf 1_R-X\|
\right).
}
\]
For a supplied braiding \(\beta_{X,Y}\),
\[
\boxed{
\Delta_{\beta}^{\otimes}(X,Y)
=
\left\|
\beta_{X,Y}(X\otimes Y)-Y\otimes X
\right\|.
}
\]
The zero-defect reference subclass is strict with respect to these checks.

\subsection{Non-strict monoidal coherence gate}

\longrightarrow

\longrightarrow

A typed relation is written

r:a\to b

and may induce transport

\boxed{
T_r:\mathcal A_a\to\mathcal A_b.

\tag{DEF-T}

No universal transport law is imposed before the relation type is supplied.

\section{Relational product: no premature associativity}

Let
\[
\star_R:
\mathcal A\times\mathcal A\to\mathcal A
\]
denote a relational product in a compatible typed sector. The current
foundation does not assume either commutativity or associativity.

Their failures are measured, not hidden:
\[
\boxed{
[X,Y]_{\star}
=X\star Y-Y\star X,
}
\]
\[
\boxed{
\alpha(X,Y,Z)
=(X\star Y)\star Z-X\star(Y\star Z).
}
\]
Commutative or associative subclasses are recovered when the corresponding
residual vanishes.

Bilinearity is also not silently assumed. Define the additive defects
\[
B_L^+(X,Y;Z)
=
(X+Y)\star Z-X\star Z-Y\star Z,
\]
\[
B_R^+(X;Y,Z)
=
X\star(Y+Z)-X\star Y-X\star Z,
\]
and scalar-linearity defects
\[
B_L^{\lambda}(X;Y)
=
(\lambda X)\star Y-\lambda(X\star Y),
\]
\[
B_R^{\lambda}(X;Y)
=
X\star(\lambda Y)-\lambda(X\star Y).
\]
Exact \(\mathbb K\)-bilinearity is therefore a zero-defect subclass:
\[
\boxed{
B_L^+=B_R^+=B_L^{\lambda}=B_R^{\lambda}=0.
}
\]
The general relational product may leave this subclass, in which case the
departure is recorded rather than hidden.

\subsection{Indexed tensor gate}

The abstract contraction interface does not by itself determine contraction
identities on tensor products. The reference element currently records total

or a canonical pairing between upper and lower indices.

Therefore expressions such as
\[
\operatorname{Contr}(X\otimes Y)
\]
remain realization-dependent until an indexed tensor model supplies
\[
\boxed{
\text{slot signature}
+
\text{pairing rule}
+
\text{component shape}
+
\text{contraction evaluator}.
}
\]
This is an explicit gate, not a missing axiom. Rank \((p,q)\) alone cannot
select which index pair is to be contracted.

\section{Semantic scales}

Semantic scale is not assumed to be a single real parameter. Let
\[
\boxed{\sigma\in\Sigma}
\]
where \(\Sigma\) is a typed partially ordered or categorical scale
structure. A coarse-graining morphism is written
\[
S_{\sigma\to\tau}:
\mathcal A_{\sigma,n}^{p,q}
\to
\mathcal A_{\tau,n}^{p,q}.
\]
Such a map is admissible only when the scale structure contains the required
relation. This prevents unrelated semantic classes from being collapsed by
arithmetic alone.

A common semantic quotient of two types exists only when there is a common
admissible upper scale. Thus semantic compatibility is part of the algebraic
type system rather than an after-the-fact annotation.

The reference kernel implements these as executable typed maps. A scale map
must be admitted by the semantic scale graph, consume an element at its source
scale, and return an element at its declared target scale while preserving the
carrier and tensor rank in the reference realization.

\section{Relational deformation potential}

The semantic scale label \(\sigma\) does not by itself determine a scalar
deformation. A concrete semantic weighting profile is supplied separately.
Let a fixed named residual signature be

\[
D(X)=\{d_i(X)\}_{i\in I},
\qquad
d_i(X)\ge 0,
\]

and let the semantic profile at \(\sigma\) assign explicit non-negative
weights

\[
W_\sigma=\{w_{\sigma i}\}_{i\in I},
\qquad
w_{\sigma i}\ge0,
\]

with at least one positive weight. The weighting law is structural input; it
is not inferred from the string or label used to name \(\sigma\). Deriving
\(W_\sigma\) from a semantic graph or contextual relation system is a
separate open realization problem and is not part of the present algebraic
definition.

For \(p\ge1\), define the relational deformation potential

\[
\boxed{
V_R^{(p)}(X\mid\sigma)
=
\left(
\sum_{i\in I}
w_{\sigma i}\,d_i(X)^p
\right)^{1/p}.
}
\]

The residual signature is part of the type contract. A potential profile is
not allowed to silently add or omit a residual component.

If the residual vector \(D(X)\) and the potential order \(p\) are held fixed
while only the semantic weighting changes, then

\[
\boxed{
\Delta_{\sigma\to\tau}V_R(X)
=
V_R^{(p)}(X\mid\tau)
-
V_R^{(p)}(X\mid\sigma)
}
\]

isolates a semantic scalar deformation. Hence two evaluations can share the
same represented algebraic state and the same numerical residuals while
having different scalar relational potential because the supplied semantic
weighting differs. A change of \(p\) is not classified as a pure semantic
shift.

For a finite admissible candidate set \(\mathcal C\), define the relational
attractor set

\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Attr}_R(\mathcal C)
=
\operatorname*{arg\,min}_{c\in\mathcal C}
V_R(c).
}
\]

Ties are preserved rather than resolved arbitrarily.

This construction is a scalar diagnostic of relational deformation. It is
not identified by the general algebra with energy, mass, gravity, or metric
curvature. A geometric realization may later supply a separate map such as

\[
\mathcal G:
V_R
\longmapsto
\delta g,
\]

but that map is an additional realization/bridge and is not assumed here.

\section{Orbital action and relative Moir\'e structure}

Let \(G\) index admissible orbital operators
\[
U_g:\mathcal A\to\mathcal A.
\]
Calling this family an exact group action requires additional coherence.
For a supplied identity representative, inverse, and product representative,
define
\[
\Delta_e(X)=\|U_eX-X\|,
\]
\[
\Delta_{\rm inv}(g;X)
=
\max\left(
\|U_g^{-1}U_gX-X\|,
\|U_gU_g^{-1}X-X\|
\right),
\]
and
\[
\Delta_G(g,h;X)
=
\|U_{gh}X-U_g(U_hX)\|.
\]
An exact group-action sector is obtained when these residuals vanish on the
domain of interest.

An orbit is derived:
\[
\boxed{
\operatorname{Orb}(X)=\{U_gX:g\in G\}.
}
\]
It is not an independent coordinate carried by \(X\).

Moir\'e structure is intrinsically relational. For two local orbital operators with a verified inverse,
\[
\boxed{
M_{ij}=U_iU_j^{-1}.
}
\tag{DER-M}
\]
In a scalar-angle realization this reduces to the wrapped relative angle
\[
\Delta\theta_{ij}
=\operatorname{wrap}_{2\pi}(\theta_i-\theta_j).
\]

Semantic scaling and orbital action need not commute. The executable mixed
residual is
\[
\boxed{
\Delta_{S,U}(X)
=
\|U(SX)-S(UX)\|.
}
\]
For a relative Moir\'e operator this specializes schematically to
\[
[M,S]=MS-SM.
\]
A non-zero value is therefore a legitimate observable of the algebra rather
than an implementation error.

\section{Relational transport and abstract holonomy}

Let
\[
\gamma=(r_1,\ldots,r_m)
\]
be a composable path of typed relations
\[
r_k:a_{k-1}\to a_k.
\]
The corresponding path transport is
\[
\boxed{
T_{\gamma}
=
T_{r_m}\circ\cdots\circ T_{r_1}.
}
\]
This definition requires no metric geometry and no phase group.

If the relation path is closed,
\[
a_m=a_0,
\]
then the relational holonomy operator is the return transport
\[
\boxed{
\mathcal H_{\gamma}:=T_{\gamma}.
}
\]
Its state-dependent residual in an additive normed reference sector is
\[
\boxed{
\varepsilon_{\gamma}(X)
=
\|\mathcal H_{\gamma}X-X\|.
}
\]
Thus a closed relation path may return to the same carrier while changing the
transported algebraic state.

When both a direct transport and a two-step transport are available, define
the composition defect
\[
\boxed{
\Delta_T(r_2,r_1;X)
=
\left\|
T_{r_2\circ r_1}X
-
T_{r_2}\bigl(T_{r_1}X\bigr)
\right\|.
}
\]
The zero-defect case is functorial transport on that composition. A non-zero
value records path/composition dependence rather than being discarded as an
implementation error.

This abstract holonomy is part of the relational core. A phase or
\(U(1)\) holonomy is a later representation of this more general transport
structure.

\section{One-axiom closure candidate}

The current canonical foundation uses two explicit axioms,
\[
\boxed{\mathrm{RA0}+\mathrm{RR1}.}
\]
The finite independence witnesses show that the original RA0 is too weak to
force recursive refinement:
\[
\boxed{\mathrm{RA0}\not\Rightarrow\mathrm{RR1}.}
\]

A stronger candidate foundational statement is therefore introduced:
\[
\boxed{
\mathrm{RA0}^{*}:
\text{every admitted carrier hosts an admitted non-degenerate binary distinction}
}
\]
together with the closure clause that the two selected endpoint occurrences
are themselves admitted carriers.

Let a relational occurrence be indexed by
\[
\omega\in\{-1,+1\}^{n}.
\]
If \(p_{\omega}\) is admitted, RA0* supplies
\[
D(p_{\omega})
=
\bigl(
p_{\omega-},
p_{\omega+}
\bigr),
\]
and both endpoints are admitted. By induction on \(n\), for every finite
depth \(N\) the complete binary address set
\[
\boxed{
V_n=\{-1,+1\}^{n},
\qquad
0\le n\le N,
}
\]
is generated. Hence
\[
\boxed{
\mathrm{RA0}^{*}\Rightarrow\mathrm{RR1}
}
\]
in the path-labelled occurrence semantics.

This is a genuine strengthening of RA0, not a proof from its old wording.
RA0* explicitly adds binary non-degeneracy and endpoint admissibility. Those
clauses are precisely what makes the induction valid.

The underlying carrier presentation may nevertheless be cyclic. For example,
\[
D(a)=(a,b),
\qquad
D(b)=(a,b)
\]
satisfies RA0* while reusing the carrier labels \(a,b\). Its unfolding is
still a complete binary tree of distinct path-labelled occurrences. Thus
recursive relational structure does not by itself imply ontically fresh
carrier identities:
\[
\boxed{
\mathrm{RA0}^{*}\not\Rightarrow\mathrm{NOVELTY}.
}
\]
If fresh carrier identity at every refinement level is required, that must be
introduced by an additional axiom or proved from additional structure.

Accordingly RA0* is recorded as a candidate axiom and
\(\mathrm{RR1}\) as a theorem candidate under RA0*. The canonical
two-axiom status is retained until this stronger wording is explicitly
promoted.

\section{Explicit non-assumptions}

The general algebra does not assume:

  \item a particular spacetime, metric, or gravitational law;
  \item associativity or commutativity of \(\star_R\);
  \item exact preservation of products under refinement;
  \item a one-dimensional numerical semantic scale;
  \item winding, orbit, or residual as independent state coordinates;
  \item a physical interpretation of an algebraic structure without a
        separate bridge theorem.
\end{itemize}
